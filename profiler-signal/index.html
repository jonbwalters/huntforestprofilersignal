A warning that arrives days before the profiler fails.

Four motion-controller channels watch the profiler infeed rolls. When their daily behaviour deviates hard for three straight production days, the signal fires. Fourteen months hold eight warning episodes — any single implementation of the rule fires on seven of them, because a threshold-edge pair swaps July for October between derivations. Seven of the eight were followed within days by a documented profiler failure or repair; the remaining one tracked rolls the mill had already documented as failing. No firing on record lacks a physical fault behind it.

What to do when it fires

On a firing
Schedule a profiler infeed inspection for the next scheduled downtime — shift change, weekend, or planned window. Check bearings, cylinders, rolls, and chains.
On a re-fire
A deviation returning while a warning is still open means the fix did not hold. Escalate before restart. December 30 and June 16–19 are the in-record examples.
The model case
Event 5, March 2026: a bearing replaced inside a window that was already dead. The episode cost essentially zero production.
The counter-case
Event 4, December 2025: the warning stood through four zero-production Christmas days, nobody inspected, and the profiler wrecked for $128k on restart.
What it is not
A guarantee. The signal is one-directional and misses roughly half of profiler failures — silence means nothing. A firing has always meant something is wrong.

How the signal is calculated and scored: Method →

Why this adds to the weekly PM

The profiler already gets a millwright inspection every Tuesday, and that route works — on the infeed rolls alone it raised twenty corrective findings in fourteen months. The failures that still got through slipped past three structural limits of any weekly walk, and the signal covers exactly those three. Each event page carries the specific instance under "Signal × weekly PM".

Cadence
An inspection every seven days has a seven-day blind window; the signal re-checks every production day. The record holds failure arcs that ran from first deviation to wreck in 5–12 days.
Aim
A visual inspection samples what looks worst; the channels are per-axis. Twice in this record a PM-raised repair fixed the mirror of the failing side — left rebuilt in November while the right failed, right roll replaced in December while the left wrecked for $128k — and both times the channels pointed at the side that was still deviating.
Verification & the queue
A deviation that persists after a CPM means the wrong element was fixed; a standing warning is the case for pulling queued work forward. In May 2026 the PM route found the rolls and scheduled the repair a month out — the warning fired four days later, and the arc ended in reactive replacements in July.

The complement runs both ways: the signal misses roughly half of profiler failures (sudden breaks do not drift first), so it aims and grades the Tuesday visit — it does not replace it.
