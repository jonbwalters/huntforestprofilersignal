A warning that arrives days before the profiler fails.

Four motion-controller channels watch the profiler infeed rolls. When any of them deviates hard for two straight production days, the signal fires. Fourteen months hold nine warnings. Eight were followed inside their window by a reactive work order, a costed stop, or a ledger repair on the profiler; the ninth was closed by a PM-raised roll replacement on the deviating side. Over the entire 14-month period that we examined, at no point did the signal fire without a physical fault subsequently appearing.  In other words, there were no false positive events — which means the signal is highly reliable.  The opposite is not true, however, sometimes a physical fault of the component occurred without tripping the rule in advance, so we shouldn't expect this rule to catch ALL failures of this component.  It will not.

What to do when it fires — the two tiers

Tier 1 — on a firing (2 days)
Aim the next Tuesday PM walk at the deviating axis, and use the next free window — shift handover, dark day, planned stop. Check bearings, cylinders, rolls, and chains on that corner. Eight of the nine warnings went live with a scheduled walk still ahead of the failure.
Tier 2 — on a third deviation day, or a re-fire
A deviation that keeps running, or returns while the warning is open, means the fix did not hold. Escalate: inspect before restart, pull queued profiler work forward. December 30 and June 16–19 are the in-record examples.
The model case
Event 8, March 2026: alarm Monday, PM walk Tuesday, a handover-sized bearing swap Thursday inside a window that was already dead. The episode cost essentially zero production.
The counter-case
Event 7, December 2025: the warning stood through four zero-production Christmas days, nobody inspected, and the profiler wrecked for $128k on restart.
What it is not
A guarantee. The signal is one-directional: it covers 11 of the 14 failure episodes on record, and the sudden breaks it misses do not drift first — silence means nothing. A firing has always meant something is wrong.

Why this adds to the weekly PM

The profiler already gets a millwright inspection every Tuesday, and that route works — on the infeed rolls alone it raised twenty corrective findings in fourteen months. It is also demonstrably insufficient alone: 12 of the 14 failure episodes had a PM visit within the seven days before onset, and the failure came anyway. The signal covers the three structural limits of any weekly walk, and the two-day rule is what makes the timing work: eight of the nine warnings went live with at least one Tuesday walk still ahead of the failure evidence, and the firings land Sunday–Tuesday in eight of nine cases — a median of one to two days before the next walk. Each event page carries the specific instance under "Signal × weekly PM".

Cadence
An inspection every seven days has a seven-day blind window; the signal re-checks every production day. The record holds failure arcs that ran from first deviation to wreck in 5–12 days.
Aim
A visual inspection samples what looks worst; the channels are per-axis. Twice in this record a PM-raised repair fixed the mirror of the failing side — left rebuilt in November while the right failed, right roll replaced in December while the left wrecked for $128k — and both times the channels pointed at the side that was still deviating.
Verification & the queue
A deviation that persists after a CPM means the wrong element was fixed; a standing warning is the case for pulling queued work forward. The spring 2026 arc is the type case: the warning fired April 26, the PM route found the rolls a month later and queued the replacement a further month out, and the arc ended in reactive replacements in July — with seven Tuesday walks inside the warning on the way.

The complement runs both ways: the signal misses roughly half of profiler failures (sudden breaks do not drift first), so it aims and grades the Tuesday visit — it does not replace it.
