What-if analysis · 2-day persistence

The same signal, firing one day sooner.

The deployed rule fires when a channel deviates for three consecutive production days. This page re-runs the whole record with a two-day requirement: every warning fires a day earlier, the fragile July 2025 episode is recovered, and every warning window closed against a documented profiler repair — paid for with nearly twice the warnings, flat-window statistics at chance level, and three windows whose only confirmation is a PM-found repair. Everything here is computed from the same series and mill records as the main site.

Method: the report's own conventions at $1,000 per output-stopped minute — costed stops claimed at 50–75% prevention, mid-shift uncosted repairs as timing shifts. Either way it is additive only if the extra warnings get acted on; as a sensitive tier it is upside, not a promise.

Verdict

What two days buys: one extra day of lead on every episode the 3-day rule already catches, and the June–July 2025 arc — a warning on June 23 that absorbed the fragile July firing and was confirmed by the July 10 cylinder repair, seventeen days later.

What it costs: nearly twice the warnings (11 vs 6), flat 7-day precision at chance level, and a thinner grade of confirmation — three of the eleven windows closed against corrective-from-PM repairs with no reactive work order, costed stop, or ledger record inside (the 3-day rule's six windows all carry strict evidence). The May 26 boundary case shows how timing-fragile verdicts get at this threshold: the window closed one day before a $16.7k profiler stop already tied to a reactive repair.

Recommendation: keep the 3-day rule as the trigger. The 2-day condition earns its keep as a sensitive tier — a note on the shift report, never a page — where the extra day of lead, the July-arc catch, and roughly $25–90k/yr of reactive time (depending on baseline, above) are worth having and the weaker confirmation grade costs nothing.
